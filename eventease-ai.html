Why this work matters

Context, purpose, and audience

Workshop context

This AI Lab focused on using a large language model, building a custom GPT concept, applying design thinking, evaluating outputs, and documenting responsible use.

Purpose and audience

EventEase AI supports student organizations whose leaders may have strong ideas but limited time, budget, planning experience, or knowledge of accessibility and approval requirements.

Problem addressed

Campus events involve connected decisions about venues, budgets, roles, technology, promotion, accessibility, and contingencies. Missing one requirement can delay or disrupt the event.

Professional relevance

The artifact demonstrates requirements gathering, risk identification, boundary definition, test design, expected-versus-observed evaluation, and controlled documentation.

Skills and learning outcomes

Build a focused AI assistant around a defined user problem instead of a general-purpose chatbot.
Write constraints that separate estimates and assumptions from confirmed facts.
Test normal, changed, emergency, and unethical-request scenarios.
Preserve accessibility, privacy, honesty, and human responsibility throughout the workflow.
Documented testing

Evidence and process

Design-thinking workflow

The project moved through empathize, define, ideate, prototype, and test. The assistant was instructed to collect essential details, label estimates, preserve the event’s core objective, reduce optional costs first, and refuse fabricated approvals, receipts, or vendor information.

Key observed results

The normal workshop scenario produced a three-week plan with an estimated total of $275, a $30 contingency reserve, and a $25 cushion. When the budget dropped, the assistant protected the main educational activity and reduced optional expenses first. When the venue became unavailable, it recommended an approved alternative, virtual delivery, postponement, or cancellation rather than an unsafe or unapproved location.

Student reflection

Reflection and self-assessment

This AI Lab changed my understanding of what makes an AI assistant useful. A strong assistant is not defined by how much content it can generate. It is defined by whether it has a clear purpose, asks for the right information, respects boundaries, adapts to changing conditions, and makes assumptions visible. The design-thinking process helped me keep EventEase AI centered on actual student needs instead of adding unnecessary features.

The testing process connected closely with my validation background because I defined expected behavior, created scenarios, compared observed results with expectations, and documented limitations. The honesty test was especially important because the assistant refused deceptive documents while still providing legitimate alternatives. A future version should provide a short priority plan first, wait for essential answers before making major assumptions, and include reusable checklists while keeping human verification and privacy controls in place.
