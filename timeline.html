The Intelligence Line

Board a route through symbolic reasoning, AI winters, expert systems, machine learning, deep learning, cloud computing, transformers, and generative AI.

Route guide

FoundationsAI wintersModern learningTransfer points

Alan Turing published “Computing Machinery and Intelligence” and introduced the imitation game, later called the Turing Test. The paper gave researchers a practical way to discuss machine intelligence.

Main players: Alan Turing

Impact: Created an early framework for evaluating intelligent behavior.

John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Claude Shannon, and Nathaniel Rochester organized the Dartmouth Summer Research Project. The proposal helped establish the term “artificial intelligence.”

Main players: McCarthy · Minsky · Shannon · Rochester

Impact: Brought researchers together around a shared field of study.

Frank Rosenblatt demonstrated the perceptron, an early trainable neural-network model. It showed that a machine could adjust internal weights from examples.

Main players: Frank Rosenblatt

Impact: Introduced a foundation for later neural-network learning.

Joseph Weizenbaum created ELIZA, a program that matched text patterns to imitate a psychotherapist. Its simple rules still convinced some users that the computer understood them.

Main players: Joseph Weizenbaum · MIT

Impact: Raised early questions about language, trust, and human attachment to machines.

SRI’s Shakey robot combined perception, planning, and movement. It was an important step toward robots that could reason about their surroundings.

Main players: SRI International

Impact: Linked symbolic planning with real-world robotic behavior.

Researchers at Stanford developed MYCIN to recommend antibiotics for bacterial infections. It used hand-written rules and demonstrated the value of specialized, narrow AI.

Main players: Stanford University

Impact: Showed how expert systems could support decisions in a focused domain.

Early promises exceeded the available computing power, data, and program capability. Funding declined when systems could not handle the complexity of real environments.

Main players: Research agencies and universities

Impact: Reduced investment and forced the field to reconsider unrealistic expectations.

Rule-based systems such as XCON helped companies configure products and preserve specialist knowledge. Commercial success brought AI back into corporate planning.

Main players: Digital Equipment Corporation · Carnegie Mellon

Impact: Moved AI from laboratories into practical business operations.

Expert systems were expensive to maintain, specialized hardware lost value, and many projects failed to meet expectations. Investment and public confidence fell again.

Main players: AI vendors · corporate adopters

Impact: Encouraged researchers to seek data-driven methods that could improve with experience.

IBM’s Deep Blue defeated world chess champion Garry Kasparov in a six-game match. Massive parallel computing and carefully designed search demonstrated machine strength in a complex task.

Main players: IBM · Garry Kasparov

Impact: Made AI achievement visible to a worldwide audience.

Geoffrey Hinton and collaborators showed improved methods for training multilayer neural networks. Larger datasets and faster processors made connectionist approaches practical again.

Main players: Geoffrey Hinton and collaborators

Impact: Renewed interest in neural networks after years of limited progress.

Fei-Fei Li and collaborators built ImageNet, a large labeled image database and benchmark. It gave computer-vision researchers a common way to train and compare models.

Main players: Fei-Fei Li · Princeton · Stanford

Impact: Demonstrated how large, organized datasets could accelerate model improvement.

IBM Watson defeated champion players on Jeopardy! by combining natural-language processing, information retrieval, ranking, and large-scale computing.

Main players: IBM Research

Impact: Showed that machines could process clues, ambiguity, and broad knowledge under time pressure.

Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, and Geoffrey Hinton used GPUs to train a deep convolutional network that won the ImageNet competition by a large margin.

Main players: Krizhevsky · Sutskever · Hinton · NVIDIA GPUs

Impact: Proved that deep networks, big data, and GPUs could outperform traditional vision methods.

Ian Goodfellow and colleagues introduced generative adversarial networks. A generator and discriminator trained against each other to produce increasingly realistic outputs.

Main players: Ian Goodfellow and collaborators

Impact: Opened a major path for synthetic images and generative modeling.

Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud expanded access to scalable storage, GPUs, machine-learning tools, and hosted APIs.

Main players: AWS · Microsoft · Google

Impact: Allowed students, startups, and enterprises to build AI without owning a supercomputer.

DeepMind’s AlphaGo won four of five games against Go champion Lee Sedol. It combined deep neural networks, search, and reinforcement learning.

Main players: Google DeepMind · Lee Sedol

Impact: Demonstrated learning and planning in a problem once considered far beyond computers.

Google researchers introduced the Transformer architecture. Its attention mechanism enabled parallel training and became the foundation for modern large language models.

Main players: Vaswani and collaborators · Google

Impact: Changed natural-language processing and later influenced vision, audio, and multimodal AI.

OpenAI released GPT-3, a 175-billion-parameter language model that could perform many tasks from instructions and examples without task-specific retraining.

Main players: OpenAI

Impact: Popularized few-shot prompting and showed how model scale could produce broad language capability.

OpenAI launched ChatGPT as a conversational interface. Generative AI rapidly entered education, software development, business, and creative work.

Main players: OpenAI · Microsoft

Impact: Turned large language models into a widely used everyday technology.

Leading systems began working across text, images, audio, and video. OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and cloud providers competed to improve capability and deployment.

Main players: OpenAI · Google · Anthropic · Meta · Microsoft · NVIDIA

Impact: Expanded AI from prediction toward content creation, assistants, coding, science, and industry workflows.

End of the line?

No. AI history shows repeated cycles of confidence and correction. The next stage will depend on technical progress, responsible use, regulation, access to computing, and human choices.
